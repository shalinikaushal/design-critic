# Product and business alignment

Does the design serve the stated user and business goals, and does it avoid trading long-term trust for short-term gain? This covers goal fit, conversion and retention drivers, value communication, ethics and measurability.

## Core principles

- **Goals are the test.** Without stated goals, product alignment is limited to what the design itself reveals.
- **User value and business value together:** a good design makes the valuable thing easy for both.
- **Honest persuasion:** persuasion that helps users decide is good; deception is not.
- **Measurability:** important outcomes can be observed, so claims can be tested.
- **Fit to context:** audience, market, regulation and platform shape what is appropriate.

## Evaluation criteria

1. Does the design make the goal-linked action (sign up, buy, return) clear and low-friction?
2. Is the value proposition visible at the moment it matters?
3. Is information that builds trust (costs, security, returns, social proof) present before commitment?
4. Does it support the retention loop (reasons to return, signals of progress)?
5. Are there dark patterns (hidden costs, forced continuity, confirm-shaming, misleading defaults)?
6. Does it respect regulation and sensitive contexts (money, health, children)?
7. Can success be measured with the information on screen?

## Common problems

- The key action is competing with promotions or secondary tasks.
- Costs and conditions revealed late.
- Dashboards with numbers but no change, target or next action.
- Onboarding that asks before it gives value.
- Upgrade prompts at moments of friction.
- Trust cues missing at payment or data entry.
- Defaults that favour the business at the user's expense.

## How to identify issues

1. Write the goals from the request in one sentence each.
2. For each goal, trace the path in the design and note friction or missing cues.
3. Ask what a sceptical user needs to know before committing.
4. Scan for manipulative patterns.

## Evidence requirements

- Tie each finding to a stated goal and quote it.
- Do **not** invent metrics, conversion rates or research. Use phrases such as "could affect", and say how it could be measured.
- Without goals, restrict findings to evident risks (hidden costs, dark patterns) and list the goals you would need as open questions.
- Industry claims ("cart abandonment is mostly caused by shipping cost") must be framed as general patterns, not facts about this product.

## Severity guide

| Severity | Alignment example |
|---|---|
| critical | The design works against a stated core goal, or is deceptive or harmful |
| major | A key goal-linked step lacks information users need before committing |
| moderate | Missed opportunity to reinforce value at a key moment |
| minor | Small copy or placement improvement for conversion |
| suggestion | Experiment idea |

## Recommended fixes (patterns)

- Surface costs and conditions earlier.
- Add context to metrics (change, target, next action).
- Move value before the ask in onboarding.
- Replace manipulative patterns with honest alternatives.
- Propose a measurement, such as an A/B test or funnel step, for the change.

## Practical example

*Observation:* KPI cards show values with no comparison or trend.
*Goal:* increase weekly active users.
*Impact:* little reason to return or act.
*Fix:* add period-over-period change and optional target.
*Confidence:* medium: depends on audience behaviour that was not tested.

## Quick checklist

- [ ] Findings tie to a stated goal
- [ ] Costs and conditions are visible before commitment
- [ ] Value is shown before the ask
- [ ] No manipulative patterns
- [ ] Success can be measured
- [ ] No invented metrics

## Edge cases and limitations

- Conflicting goals (growth vs trust): state the conflict and recommend the option that protects long-term trust.
- Internal tools: replace conversion with task efficiency and adoption.
- Regulated domains: flag possible regulatory sensitivity as an open question and avoid legal conclusions.
- Business context is usually the thinnest input; keep confidence modest.
