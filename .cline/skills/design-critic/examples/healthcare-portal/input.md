# Example input: patient portal (information architecture focus)

This example shows a multi-screen review where the main problems are about **structure and labelling**, not visual styling.

**Artifacts (illustrative, described in text so the example is self-contained):**

- `portal-home` (1440x900): left navigation with eight items in this order: Home, Appointments, Records, Messages, Account, Profile, Settings, Help. A "Help" dropdown at the top right lists FAQs, Contact us and Billing & payments. The main area shows upcoming appointments.
- `account` (1440x900): page heading "Details". Sections: Personal information, Insurance, Payment methods, Password. No item in the left navigation has a selected style. No balance or billing link is shown.
- `settings` (1440x900): page heading "Preferences". Sections: Notifications, Language, Payment reminders, Insurance sharing. No item in the left navigation has a selected style.
- `billing` (1440x900): breadcrumb "Help / Billing & payments", page heading "Statement center", a balance due of an outstanding amount and a "Pay now" button.

**Journey:** A patient wants to update their insurance and pay an outstanding balance. They look at the navigation, open Account, check Settings, and eventually reach billing through Help.

**Platform:** web. **Audience:** adult patients, many of them occasional users.

**Goals:** increase on-time bill payment; reduce calls to the support line about where to find things.

**Request:** "Patients keep phoning us to ask where things are. Review the portal structure."

**Not supplied:** the Profile page, a sitemap, search, analytics, or any interaction states.
